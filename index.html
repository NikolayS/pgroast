Find out why your Postgres proposal might not be accepted — before you post it.

PG Roast is an adversarial reviewer for Postgres proposals. It judges the idea and the design, not the code. Given a pgsql-hackers thread or a CommitFest entry, it argues the way experienced committers do: earlier attempts that were not accepted, the strongest objections with concrete failure scenarios, and the touch points that raise the risk. A critic then tries to refute every objection, and only the survivors are reported.

Prepared by an AI harness; treat as a devil's advocate, not a verdict. Output is local files only. Nothing is posted to mailing lists or to CommitFest, and there is no code that could do so.

Who it is for

Proposals die on pgsql-hackers for reasons their authors could have anticipated: an idea tried and rejected before, a design that trips a known touch point, demand nobody showed.

✍️ Author self-check primary

Run pgroast <my-thread-url> before posting. Get the strongest surviving objections and the prior attempts, then reframe or drop the proposal privately.

🧭 Precedent sanity v0.1

If your idea resembles an old rejected patch that eventually landed, that precedent is marked later-accepted and is not cited against you.

⏱️ Triage saver

A committer runs pgroast on a CommitFest entry and gets a ranked, critic-survived objection list in a few minutes, so review time goes to the real risks.

📊 Batch calibration v0.1

The maintainer runs pgroast batch <commitfest> over a closed CommitFest to compare verdicts with real outcomes and measure agreement.

What you get

Four files in a local directory: roast.md (the short report), roast-full.md, roast.json and roast-summary.txt.

Neutral restatement

What the proposal actually asks for, stated without spin.

Prior attempts

From the precedent index, with links, status and who objected.

Ranked objections

Only those that survived the critic, each with a failure scenario and what the author would need to show.

Touch points

All 15, each marked hit or not hit.

Questions for the author

Up to 5.

Verdict with confidence

Plus three fields kept apart from it: committability, not_now and priority.

Refuted objections stay in the JSON and are listed at the end of the full report with the critic's reason. All prose follows one set of writing rules: the point first, hard length limits per field, plain words.

Verdict scale

The verdict covers the review pyramid from the base up: 1a understandability, 1b desirability, 2 architecture and design. When a level fails, the levels above it are not argued. Implementation is out of scope.

LIKELY-REJECT

A fatal objection at the idea or design level survived the critic and cannot be overcome without making a different proposal, or the same idea was not accepted before and nothing has changed.

NEEDS-DESIGN-DISCUSSION

The goal may be fine, but the design or the demand is in question; the list needs to agree on something before code review makes sense. "Not now, show demand" goes here.

NEEDS-COMMITTER-EYES

No fundamental objection to the idea, but it touches things that only a committer who knows the area can sign off, or the remaining concerns are fixable design points.

NO-FUNDAMENTAL-OBJECTION

Nothing that survived would stop it at the idea or design level. This says nothing about the code.

How it works

The models have no tools and never see a URL: they cite ids such as P3, and code fills in the links from the database. Quotes are accepted only as verbatim substrings of the author's own text. Free prose is not fact-checked by code.

Outcome-aware precedents v0.1 · planned

The worst failure for an author is being told "this was rejected before" about an idea that was later accepted: it is wrong, it is discouraging, and it destroys trust in one shot. v0.1 gives every precedent an outcome and adds a revival guard. Pick an outcome to see how a precedent with it may be used.

Outcome field

Populated from the CommitFest end status plus a git pass that links a reverted or returned patch to any later commit implementing the same feature, by thread lineage and message-id chains. Unresolvable becomes unknown.

Revival guard

A "tried before and rejected" objection is dropped when its grounding precedent is superseded or later-accepted. Dropped objections are kept in roast.json with reason precedent-later-accepted and listed at the end of roast-full.md.

Calibration

batch gains a post-pass that joins each entry's verdict to its real CommitFest outcome and writes an agreement table, calibration.json and calibration.md, to the output directory. No outcome is fetched from the network beyond the loaded index.

Scope

English pgsql-hackers threads and CommitFest entries. Out of scope: posting anything anywhere, non-Postgres proposals, non-English threads.

Get started

A roast is 12 to 20 model calls, made through an AI coding CLI that is signed in with your subscription. No API key is needed or used.


    

    

      
Requirements

        
    
          
  • An AI coding CLI, installed and signed in: Claude Code or Codex CLI. Calls count toward that account's limits.
    • 
          
  • Bun 1.3.5 or later. There are no npm dependencies.
    • 
          
  • Postgres 18, for the precedent index (database pgroast; PGROAST_DSN if it is not on the local socket).
    • 
          
  • A clone of the Postgres repository, only if you rebuild the index (PGROAST_PG_REPO).
    • 
        

      

      
Keys stay out

        
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN, OPENAI_API_KEY and CODEX_API_KEY are removed from the CLI's environment, and a call fails if the CLI reports that it is using a key.
